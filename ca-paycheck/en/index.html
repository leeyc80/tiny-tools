Canada Take-Home Pay Calculator 2026

Enter an hourly wage or salary to see what lands in your bank account after tax, CPP and EI.

This is an estimate. It assumes basic TD1 claim amounts and does not cover Quebec. For exact payroll deductions use the CRA Payroll Deductions Online Calculator (PDOC).

What comes off your paycheque?

  • CPP – 5.95% of earnings above $3,500, up to $4,230.45 in 2026. Earnings between $74,600 and $85,000 also pay 4% CPP2 (max $416).
  • EI – 1.63% of insurable earnings, up to $1,123.07 in 2026.
  • Federal income tax – 14%, 20.5%, 26%, 29% and 33% brackets for 2026, with a basic personal amount of $16,452.
  • Provincial income tax – Ontario starts at 5.05% and adds the Ontario Health Premium; BC starts at 5.6% with a low-income tax reduction; Alberta starts at 8% with a $22,769 basic personal amount.

How accurate is it?

It applies the 2026 CRA payroll formulas (T4127) and basic TD1 amounts on an annual basis, assuming the same pay all year. If you started mid-year or your hours vary, your actual pay stubs will differ, and any over-deducted tax is refunded when you file your return. BC's July 2026 rate change is reflected using the full-year values.

Vacation pay and overtime

Hourly employees in Ontario, BC and Alberta usually receive 4% vacation pay (6% after five years). Choose 4% if it is added to each paycheque. Overtime is paid at 1.5× after 44 hours a week in Ontario and Alberta, and after 8 hours a day or 40 a week in BC.
